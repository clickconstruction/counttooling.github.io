Whoever services a rooftop unit or a walk-in compressor has to be able to see the switch that kills it. So the code puts a disconnect within sight of the equipment and where it can be reached without a portable ladder or tools: on a rooftop unit, a disconnect on the roof beside it, whatever breaker feeds it from the panel.

On a bid it is a line item the one-line may not draw: the disconnect itself, rated for the unit, the conduit and wire to it and from it, and the roof work to mount it.

## What the app does with it

The electrical course teaches it on a card (Chapter 6, Poles), and `check-lesson-rules` holds the card's numbers to this rule. The app counts a disconnect you mark; it does not ask whether each unit has one.

## Verify against your edition

Within sight is a defined term: visible, and not more than 50 ft away. Readily accessible means reached without a portable ladder or tools, and since the 2020 edition a key does not count as a tool. The section's exception for a remote disconnect that can be locked open is for equipment essential to an industrial process, with written safety procedures and qualified service staff, never a restaurant's rooftop unit. Local amendments still govern.
