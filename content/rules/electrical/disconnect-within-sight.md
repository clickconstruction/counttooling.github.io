Whoever services a rooftop unit or a walk-in compressor has to be able to see the switch that kills it. So the code puts a disconnect within sight of the equipment and where it can be reached without a ladder or a key: on a rooftop unit, a disconnect on the roof beside it, whatever breaker feeds it from the panel.

On a bid it is a line item the one-line may not draw: the disconnect itself, rated for the unit, the conduit and wire to it and from it, and the roof work to mount it.

## What the app does with it

Nothing yet. The electrical course teaches it on a card (Chapter 6, Poles). The app counts a disconnect you mark; it does not ask whether each unit has one.

## Verify against your edition

Within sight is a defined term, visible and not more than 50 ft away, and the section has exceptions for some equipment and for disconnects that can be locked open. A tester signs it against the adopted edition before this rule is applied.
