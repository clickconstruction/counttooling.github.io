A hood pulls a great deal of air out of one room. On the course's restaurant EF-1 takes 2,400 CFM out of a kitchen that the rooftop unit feeds 800, and that air has to come from somewhere. The code's answer is make-up air: outside air supplied to replace what the hood exhausts, running whenever the hood does. Leave it out and the kitchen goes negative: doors pull hard, the hood spills smoke instead of capturing it, and the gas appliances starve for combustion air.

On a bid it is the line that gets forgotten, because it is a second unit (the make-up air unit, often tempered so the cooks are not standing in a winter draft), its own duct and register, and an interlock so it starts with the exhaust fan. The interlock is controls wiring, and whose wiring it is belongs in an RFI with the electrician.

## What the app does with it

Nothing automatic yet. You count the make-up air unit and its register and trace its duct like any supply run, and they price on the Duct Schedule with the rest. Bid Check's Controls row is where you say who owns the interlock. The app does not compare a hood's exhaust with the make-up air you traced.

## Verify against your edition

The course cites IMC 508 as a whole. Check which subsection your adopted edition puts the interlock in, and whether it limits how far the make-up air temperature may differ from the room, since that decides whether the make-up unit is tempered and what it costs.
