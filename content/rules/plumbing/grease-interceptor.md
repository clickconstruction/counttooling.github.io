In a grease interceptor the kitchen's waste slows down, the grease floats and cools, and a truck pumps it out on a schedule. Only waste that carries grease belongs in it: the three-compartment sinks, the dish pit, the floor drains and floor sinks in the kitchen and the bar. Sanitary waste from the restrooms would fill it with what it cannot separate, so the restrooms go around it and join the sewer downstream.

On a bid that is two waste systems, each with its own pipe to the sewer, and the interceptor itself: a large precast or steel tank outside, with its excavation, its covers and its sampling point, often priced on its own line.

## What the app does with it

Nothing yet: the app does not apply this rule. It is on a course card (the plumbing course, Chapter 4, "Waste on its own layer"), which has you trace the grease line and the sanitary line apart. The app does not know which fixtures carry grease or check what drains through the interceptor.

## Verify against your edition

The IPC section requires the interceptor for grease-laden waste; read the edition your jurisdiction adopts for which fixtures it names and which it excuses. The plain statement that a water closet or urinal must not drain through one is the Uniform Plumbing Code's (1014.1 in recent editions); under the IPC it follows from the interceptor serving grease waste, and the local sewer authority usually says it too. Interceptor sizing is the sewer authority's and the engineer's, not this rule's.
