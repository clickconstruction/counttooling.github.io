The prep sink and the dishwasher on the course's restaurant do not connect to the waste pipe. Each ends above a floor sink with open air between the end of its pipe and the rim, so when the sewer backs up it fills the floor sink and spills onto the floor, where someone sees it, instead of rising into a bowl that food touches or a machine that washes plates.

On a bid that is two things the plan may not draw: the floor sink, a fixture with its own trap, and the indirect drain from the equipment down to it, pipe you run. The floor sink then drains like any other fixture, so where it sees little water it needs seal protection too (plumb.trap.seal).

## What the app does with it

Nothing yet: the app does not apply this rule. It is on a course card (the plumbing course, Chapter 2, "What the FD keynote costs"), which has you count the floor sinks as their own counter. No surface warns about a food fixture drawn straight into the waste.

## Verify against your edition

The sub-sections under 802 (food handling and commercial dishwashers under 802.1, the air gap and the air break after it) are numbered differently from edition to edition; read 802 in the one your jurisdiction adopts. The course card says an air gap for both the prep sink and the dishwasher. The IPC allows an air break for a commercial dishwasher, and a local health code may still ask for the gap. The Uniform Plumbing Code covers the same ground in its Chapter 8, with its own figures.
