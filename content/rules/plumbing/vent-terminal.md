A waste stack keeps going past its last fixture and out through the roof, open at the top, so air can move in and out as water falls down it. The terminal sits above the roof far enough that snow and roofing do not close it, and clear of the doors, windows and air intakes that would pull sewer gas back into the building.

On a bid each terminal is a roof penetration: the pipe above the roof, a flashing and, usually, the roofer's work around it. The course's riser shows the stack a foot above the roof, and the plan shows only a circle marked VTR.

## What the app does with it

Nothing yet: the app does not apply this rule. It is on a course card (the plumbing course, Chapter 5, "Why the stack keeps going"). The app does not place or check vent terminals; you count the VTRs the plan shows and trace the stack from the riser.

## Verify against your edition

The IPC leaves the height above the roof blank for the adopting jurisdiction to fill in, and the numbers adopted vary (6 inches, 12 inches, more where snow is deep); the course's foot is a common figure, not the model code's. The Uniform Plumbing Code sets 6 inches above the roof and the same 10 foot and 3 foot clearances (906 in recent editions). The sub-section for the location clearances has moved between editions.
