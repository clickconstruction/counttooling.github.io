The Basics

Everything a first takeoff needs — upload to export, in order.

By the end you can take a plan PDF from upload to a priced-ready export: scale set and verified, fixtures counted, runs measured, mistakes fixed, and the numbers delivered.

1 How to do a takeoff from a PDF Step-by-step — upload a plan PDF, set the scale, count fixtures, measure runs, and export a report, all in your browser with CountTooling. 2 Preparing a plan set Get a bid set ready to count — upload PDFs, trim to just your sheets, rotate and rename pages, and add addendum sheets without losing work. 3 Setting the scale on a plan Calibrate a PDF so measurements are accurate — set scale from two points, use architectural/engineering presets, or enter a custom scale. 4 Is your scale lying to you? Compressed PDFs silently break preset scales. How CountTooling detects rescaled sheets, corrects presets, and lets you verify a scale before you trust a bid to it. 5 Counting fixtures with counters Place point counts on a plan with custom icons and colors, keep a live tally, and organize them into groups — the heart of any takeoff. 6 Measuring runs with lines and polylines Trace pipe, conduit, and other linear runs to get real lengths off the plan — straight lines, multi-segment polylines, arcs, drops, and the Measure tool. 7 Fixing mistakes and editing marks Undo 50 steps, clear a whole region with Delete Area, edit any mark from its right-click menu, and rename or recolor an entire type at once. 8 Reports, exports, and sending a takeoff onward Turn your marked-up plan into numbers and deliverables — the on-canvas legend, Show Report, Export PDFs, Copy to PipeTooling, and email summaries.
