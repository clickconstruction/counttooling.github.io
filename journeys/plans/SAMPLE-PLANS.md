## 1. The distinction, in the app's terms

Design-build is the path where the drawing carries no engineered system and the
estimator designs while bidding: rooms → CFM → duct-by-size (DUCT-PLAN), fixtures →
WSFU → pipe size at `S` (WATER-PLAN), receptacles → circuits and homeruns (Electrical,
First-Class S4). Teaching the app is the other path: reading what an engineer already
drew, which exercises scale, counters, tags, sized lines, zones, layers, pages and the
hand-off.

So the two sheets want opposite things:

| | Design-build sheet (today: "simple") | App-use set (today: "advanced") |
|---|---|---|
| Carries | the **inputs** the design math reads | **one of everything** the app knows how to read |
| Omits | every answer: no piping, duct, circuits | nothing the tools need; engineered and busy |
| Discipline | architectural (A-101), one sheet, one scale | plumbing + electrical + mechanical, a small set |
| Lesson shape | "design the system on this plan" | "take off what is drawn" |
| Success | the Bid Check goes green from work the student did | every tool was used once and the hand-off is complete |

## 2. The design-build sheet (promote candidate A)

Candidate A is already the right kind of drawing: an architectural sheet with room
names and numbers, doors, a corridor, a legend and general notes, and no MEP. It
stays free of piping, duct and circuits. What it needs is the inputs and the starting
points:

- **Room inputs the duct model reads.** Room names that match `ROOM_TYPES` in
  duct-model.js (office, conference, break, storage; the lobby, corridor and
  restrooms as custom) and a **room schedule** on the sheet: number, name, area,
  ceiling height, occupancy. The HVAC lesson boxes rooms the plan names (X4 option A
  labels once per room) and takes CFM from type × area.
- **Fixture inputs the water slice reads.** Fixture counts per restroom as drawn
  (WCs, lavs, urinals, the mop sink, drinking fountains, a break-room sink), a
  "public occupancy" note, and the **water heater** and **service entry** marked, so
  the plumbing lesson has a root for its cold and hot runs and a size to step down
  from.
- **Electrical starting points.** A panel location (LP-1, its pole count in a note),
  the service entry, and a reflected-ceiling note or a lighting count per room, so the
  electrical lesson can place receptacles, chain with the ceiling height and tag a
  circuit that ends at a real panel.
- **Mechanical starting point.** A roof note naming the unit and its capacity
  (RTU-1, 2000 CFM — the figure the HVAC tour already uses), and a shaft or chase
  location, so the duct lesson's main has somewhere to start.
- **A note that says the job.** "MEP DESIGN-BUILD BY CONTRACTOR — SIZE AND ROUTE PER
  CODE" in the general notes: the sheet tells the student why nothing is drawn.
- **One sheet, one scale, no zones.** A design lesson should not spend its first
  minutes on page handling; multiply and scale zones belong on the app-use set.

Open drawing items from the 2026-09-14 review: the lobby's exterior door swings
outside the building line; the corridor drinking fountains sit under the conference
wall rather than in a recess.

## 3. The app-use set (grow the advanced sheet into three)

The round-2 restaurant sheet (P-101: cook line under its hood with gas drops, dish pit
with a FOH pass-thru, sized CW/HW/G runs, keytags, a legend) is the first sheet of the
set. Each sheet exists to exercise tools on purpose:

| Sheet | Teaches | Must carry |
|---|---|---|
| **P-101 plumbing** (exists) | scale, counters + keytags, sized line types, polylines, Bid Check, hand-off | title-block scale + graphic scale + a dimension to prove it; fixtures with keytags; CW/HW/G runs with sizes the Quick Line parses; a **fixture schedule table** for the schedule-box tool; one **"TYP. OF 4" booth or bay** for a multiply zone; an **enlarged restroom detail at 1/4"** for a scale zone |
| **E-101 electrical** (new) | Trade = electrical, tags, circuits, homeruns, panel schedule, conductors | receptacles/switches with mount notes, lettered **fixture types with a schedule** (the S6 tag reader), a panel **LP-1** with a **panel schedule**, homerun arrows with circuit tags, a conduit legend with sizes |
| **M-101 mechanical** (new) | duct by size, diffusers with CFM, equipment groups, pounds | RTU-1 on a roof key, a drawn duct main with size callouts, diffusers with CFM tags, a room schedule with CFM, an air-balance note |

Set-level: three pages in one PDF (pages tool, per-sheet reports), room names in the
text layer on every sheet (room labels), the same title block and scale on all three
(scale carries page to page), and one sheet at a different scale (the enlarged detail)
so Set Scale's per-page path is exercised. Two **layers** on P-101 (water on one,
waste/vent on another) teach the layer switcher and layer-aware copy (X6).

## 4. What follows in the app

- **Tours move.** The three app-use tours (`ELECTRICAL_STEPS`, `PLUMBING_STEPS`,
  `HVAC_STEPS` in features/tutorial.js) re-target the set: electrical onto E-101,
  plumbing onto P-101, HVAC onto M-101; `openSamplePlan` becomes `openSampleSet` with
  a page index per tour. Coordinates re-pin once.
- **Design lessons arrive.** One per trade on the design-build sheet, on the same
  engine (`TOURS` map, do-it-for-me per step): HVAC — box the rooms, set the CFM,
  trace the main, read the pounds; plumbing — count the fixtures, set occupancy,
  trace cold and hot, take the size at `S`, read the schedule (after WATER-PLAN rung 4);
  electrical — place receptacles, set the ceiling, chain, circuit to LP-1, Bid Check.
- **Names stop lying.** In the empty-canvas hint (`#canvasEmptyHint`), Project Settings
  (`#settingsAdvancedPlan`) and the guides: **"design-build sample"** and **"sample set
  (engineered)"** say what each is for; "simple" and "advanced" go.
- **Guides follow.** `how-to-do-a-pdf-takeoff` and the three trade guides name the
  set; a new short guide, "Design-build on the sample", fronts the lessons.
- **Specs.** `advanced-sample-plan.spec.js` grows into a set spec (three pages, text
  layer per page, the schedule table parses, the multiply zone tallies); the design
  sheet gets a spec that the room schedule's rooms box to the schedule's areas within
  tolerance.
