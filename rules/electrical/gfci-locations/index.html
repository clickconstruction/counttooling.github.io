GFCI receptacle locations, non-dwelling

The rooms of a commercial building where the code wants every receptacle ground-fault protected, as the course counts them in a restaurant: restrooms, kitchens, and any receptacle within 6 ft of a sink.

Amendments

No state or local amendment to this rule is on file. Not on file means the model code as written — check your jurisdiction.

A model code the authority having jurisdiction adopts.

Outside a dwelling the code still wants people protected where water and receptacles meet. The course counts three of its locations on a restaurant plan: every receptacle in a restroom, every receptacle in a kitchen (a bar with a sink and food preparation counts), and any other receptacle within 6 ft of a sink, like the one beside the mop sink. A receptacle in the dining room or in storage, with no sink near it, stays a plain duplex.

On a bid a GFCI receptacle costs several times a duplex, so the split is money, and a receptacle the engineer drew without the GFI in a room the code protects is an RFI and a GFCI in the bid until the answer comes back.

What the app does with it

Nothing yet. The electrical course teaches it on a card (Chapter 2, The one the engineer missed). The Quick tab offers a GFCI receptacle variant, but the app does not decide which receptacles need one.

Verify against your edition

The list under 210.8(B) grows with each edition (rooftops, outdoors, damp locations, locker rooms and more), and its item numbers have shifted between editions. The three items above are the ones the course names, numbered as the card numbers them. A tester signs them against the adopted edition before this rule is applied.
