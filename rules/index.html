The rulebook

Every public trade rule the app applies, in the words it applies it — with the section it comes from. 52 rules across 3 trades. Company practice is not here; that lives with your pricing.

Electrical

Conductor ampacity
The current a conductor may carry, read from the ampacity table in the column its terminals allow: #3/0 copper carries 200 A at 75 °C, which is why a 200 A feeder is #3/0.
CodeNEC · 310.16Draft
Conduit fill limits
How much of a raceway's inside area conductors may fill — 53% for one, 31% for two, 40% for three or more.
CodeNEC · Chapter 9
Disconnect within sight of the unit
Air-conditioning and refrigeration equipment gets a disconnect within sight of it and readily reached, so the rooftop unit's circuit ends in a disconnect on the roof.
CodeNEC · 440.14Draft
Means of egress illumination on emergency power
The building code's side of the exit lights: the path out stays lit on emergency power for 90 minutes when the normal power fails.
CodeIBC · 1008.3Draft
Grounding electrode conductor size
The conductor from the service to the water pipe and the ground rods is sized from the service conductors, and #3/0 copper service conductors want a #4 copper grounding electrode conductor.
CodeNEC · 250.66Draft
Emergency lighting battery duration
How long an emergency lighting source must carry its load with the normal power out, 90 minutes, which is why every exit sign and emergency light on a small job carries a battery.
CodeNEC · 700.12Draft
EMT bends between pull points
A run of EMT may turn no more than 360° in total between pull points, the equal of four quarter bends, so a run with more needs a box or conduit body in it.
CodeNEC · 358.26Draft
EMT support spacing
Where EMT is strapped, within 3 ft of every box or termination and at least every 10 ft along the run, which is the strap count on a takeoff.
CodeNEC · 358.30Draft
Equipment grounding conductor size
The ground that rides with a feeder is sized from the breaker ahead of it, not from the phase wires, and a 200 A breaker wants a #6 copper ground.
CodeNEC · 250.122Draft
Circuits for fixed equipment
The code limits what may share a branch circuit with equipment fastened in place, which is why the dishwasher, the pump and the rooftop unit each get a circuit of their own.
CodeNEC · 210.23Draft
GFCI receptacle locations, non-dwelling
The rooms of a commercial building where the code wants every receptacle ground-fault protected, as the course counts them in a restaurant: restrooms, kitchens, and any receptacle within 6 ft of a sink.
CodeNEC · 210.8Draft
Shunt trip on hood suppression
When a kitchen hood's fire-suppression system fires, the electric power to the cooking equipment under it shuts off by itself, usually through a shunt-trip breaker wired to the system.
StandardNFPA 96 · 10.4.1Draft
Make-up on a vertical
The foot of conduit the app adds to every vertical it writes, for the box entry and the bend that plan view never shows.
Conventiontrade practice · estimating convention
Default mount heights for devices
The heights above finished floor the app starts each device at — 18" receptacles, 44" GFCI at a counter, 48" switches, 78" panel top — so the Chain tool can write the vertical.
ConventionNEC · 404.8
Occupant sensor lighting controls
The energy code wants the lights in rooms people leave, restrooms, storage and break rooms among them, on sensors that turn them off by themselves.
CodeIECC · C405.2.1Draft
Service and feeder load calculation
A service or feeder is sized by the engineer's load calculation under Article 220, never less than the calculated load, and in practice with room for what the owner adds later.
CodeNEC · Article 220Draft
Small conductor protection
The largest breaker the code lets protect the small copper sizes, 15 A on #14, 20 A on #12 and 30 A on #10, which is why a 20 A circuit is #12.
CodeNEC · 240.4Draft
The K constant for voltage drop
The resistivity figure the estimator's single-phase voltage-drop formula uses — 12.9 for copper, 21.2 for aluminum, in ohm-circular-mils per foot.
ConventionNEC · Chapter 9
Voltage drop on a branch circuit
The app flags a branch circuit whose drop to the farthest device exceeds 3% — the NEC's informational recommendation, not a requirement.
RecommendationNEC · 210.19
Working space clearance
The clear space the code keeps in front of a panel someone may work on live, as the course teaches it at 120/208 V: 36 in deep, 30 in wide, 6 ft 6 in high.
CodeNEC · 110.26Draft

HVAC

Diffuser neck velocity
A diffuser's neck is picked from the maker's sound and throw data and the push its flex needs; makers keep neck velocity under about 1,000 feet a minute, and the app's own neck suggestion puts up to 150 CFM on an 8 in neck and up to 300 on a 10 in, so the neck size is the flex and tap size on the bid.
Conventiontrade practice · diffuser selection from the maker's catalogued soundDraft
Duct gauge by size and pressure class
The simplified gauge schedule the Duct tool picks metal with — by the duct's larger side or diameter and the system's pressure class — before you override it.
StandardSMACNA · HVAC Duct Construction Standards
Duct Schedule factors
The estimating knobs the Duct Schedule starts with — seam and waste, the quick-bid fitting factor, the design friction rate and velocity cap the ductulator suggests sizes from, and the terminal allowance the static-path check adds at the end of the critical path.
Conventiontrade practice · estimating convention
Fire dampers at rated walls
Where a duct passes through a fire-resistance-rated wall, the code wants a listed fire damper at the penetration unless an exception removes it, so each crossing of a rated wall is a damper, an access door and a sleeve on the bid.
CodeIMC · 607.5 Where requiredDraft
Grease duct metal and its weight
A commercial kitchen hood's exhaust duct is welded carbon steel of at least 16 gauge or stainless of at least 18 gauge, never the galvanized gauge schedule, so the Duct tool prices a grease run at that fixed gauge and its own sheet weight.
CodeIMC · 506.3.1.1 Grease duct materials
Make-up air for a kitchen hood
A commercial kitchen hood that exhausts air needs make-up air brought in to replace it, delivered while the hood runs and interlocked with its exhaust fan, so a hood on a plan means a second unit, its duct and its controls on the bid.
CodeIMC · 508.1 Makeup airDraft
Outdoor air for ventilation
Occupied spaces get a code minimum of outdoor air, set by the kind of room, the people in it and its floor area (a dining room 7.5 CFM a person plus 0.18 a square foot); on a bid the question is whether the plan's supply already carries it or a separate unit does.
CodeIMC · 403 Mechanical ventilationDraft
Room airflow by type
The cubic feet per minute per square foot the app assumes for a room type when no target is given — office 1.0, conference and break 1.5, storage 0.5.
Conventiontrade practice · light-commercial supply-air rule of thumb
Galvanized sheet weight by gauge
Pounds per square foot of galvanized steel sheet at each gauge — the basis of every duct weight the schedule reports.
StandardASTM · A653 / A924

Plumbing

Cleanouts on drainage piping
A drain gets a cleanout wherever a snake has to go in, at the turns of more than 45 degrees, where the building drain meets the sewer, and at least every 100 feet along a horizontal run.
CodeIPC · 708.1 Cleanouts requiredDraft
Building drain and sewer capacity in drainage fixture units
How many drainage fixture units a building drain or sewer may carry by size and slope; at 1/8 inch per foot a 3 inch sewer carries 36 and the course's 4 inch carries 180.
CodeIPC · 710.1Draft
Slope of horizontal drainage pipe
Horizontal waste pipe falls at least 1/4 inch per foot at 2-1/2 inch and smaller, 1/8 inch per foot at 3 to 6 inch, and 1/16 inch per foot at 8 inch and larger.
CodeIPC · 704.1Draft
Appliance shutoff valves
Every gas appliance has its own shutoff valve, in the same room and within 6 feet of it, upstream of its connector, so one appliance can come off the line without shutting the rest.
CodeIFGC · 409.5 Appliance shutoff valveDraft
Gas pipe sizing
Each section of gas pipe is sized for the load it carries downstream, in Btu per hour, over the longest run from the meter to the farthest outlet, so like water it shrinks as it goes.
CodeIFGC · 402 Pipe sizingDraft
Grease interceptors
Kitchen, dish and bar fixtures that carry grease drain through a grease interceptor, and the restrooms join the sewer downstream of it, because the interceptor is for grease-laden waste only.
CodeIPC · 1003.3 Grease interceptorsDraft
Hanger spacing for cast-iron pipe
How far apart cast-iron pipe may be supported — 5 feet horizontal (10 feet where 10-foot lengths are installed), 15 feet vertical.
CodeIPC · 308.5
Hanger spacing for copper tube
How far apart copper tube may be supported — 6 feet horizontal at 1-1/4 inch and smaller, 10 feet at 1-1/2 inch and larger, 10 feet vertical.
CodeIPC · 308.5
Hanger spacing for PEX
How far apart PEX tubing may be supported — 32 inches horizontal at 1 inch and smaller, 48 inches at 1-1/4 inch and larger, 10 feet vertical.
CodeIPC · 308.5
Hanger spacing for PVC pipe
How far apart PVC pipe may be supported — 4 feet horizontal, 10 feet vertical.
CodeIPC · 308.5
Hanger spacing for steel pipe
How far apart steel pipe may be supported under the plumbing code: 12 feet horizontal, 15 feet vertical.
CodeIPC · 308.5Draft
Gas shutoff tripped by the hood suppression system
When a kitchen hood's fire suppression system discharges, the gas to every appliance under the hood shuts off automatically, and stays off until someone resets it by hand, so the gas line carries a valve the system trips.
StandardNFPA 96 · 10.4 Fuel and electric power shutoffDraft
Indirect waste for food equipment
A sink or machine that handles food drains through an air gap to a floor sink or other receptor, never straight into the waste pipe, so a sewer backup rises onto the floor and not into the food side.
CodeIPC · 802 Indirect wastesDraft
Trap arms
How far a fixture's trap may sit from its vent along the drain, by trap size: 5 feet at 1-1/4 inch, 6 feet at 1-1/2 inch, 8 feet at 2 inch, 12 feet at 3 inch, 16 feet at 4 inch.
CodeIPC · 909.1Draft
Trap seal depth and protection
Every fixture trap holds a water seal 2 to 4 inches deep, and a trap that can dry out, a floor drain that sees no water for months, gets seal protection such as a trap primer.
CodeIPC · 1002.4 Trap sealsDraft
Vent terminals
A vent stack ends open above the roof, a foot up as the course teaches it, and clear of air intakes, at least 10 feet away from a door, window or intake unless it rises 3 feet above the top of it.
CodeIPC · 903 Vent terminalsDraft
Venting protects every trap seal
The drainage system carries vent piping that lets air in and out, so no fixture trap sees more than 1 inch of water column of pressure difference and no seal siphons out when the fixture upstream drains.
CodeIPC · 901.2 Trap seal protectionDraft
Minimum water service size
The water service pipe from the main to the building is never smaller than 3/4 inch; the pipes past it are sized to the fixtures they serve.
CodeIPC · 603.1
Minimum fixture supply pipe sizes
The smallest pipe that may supply each fixture, 3/8 inch for a lavatory or a tank water closet up to 1 inch for a flush-valve water closet.
CodeIPC · 604.4
Inside diameters of water pipe by nominal size
The inside diameter the velocity is computed from, per nominal size, for PEX (SDR 9), copper Type L, CPVC CTS and Schedule 40 galvanized steel.
StandardASTM · F876
Design velocity for water pipe
The velocity the app sizes a water run to, 8 feet per second cold and 5 feet per second hot. Design practice, not a code table; the code's own method sizes by pressure and length.
ConventionASPE · Plumbing Engineering Design Handbook
Demand curve: fixture units to gallons per minute
The design flow a load of water supply fixture units stands for, in gallons per minute, read off the code's demand table in its flush-tank and flush-valve columns.
CodeIPC · Appendix E
Water supply fixture units by fixture
The load a fixture puts on the water supply, in water supply fixture units (WSFU), cold and hot, by the private or public column and the kind of supply control.
CodeIPC · Appendix E
